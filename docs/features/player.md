# Player

The mini player above the tab bar and the full-screen player.

## Purpose

Play music. Pressing a track row in an album, a playlist, the popular songs of an artist or a search
song plays its list from that track. The mini player shows what plays and opens the player, which
has the seek bar, previous, play or pause, next, shuffle and repeat one. A handle at the bottom of
the player pulls up a sheet with three tabs: Up next (the rest of the queue, then, with autoplay on, a divider and the source's suggestions, under an autoplay switch), Lyrics (synced lyrics
follow the song and a line tap seeks) and Related (songs, artists, albums). A tap on a row of the rest of the queue jumps
inside the current queue, keeping its order, shuffle and source; a tap on a suggestion plays the suggestions from it, under the source "Songs like <the title of the track the source started on>"; the suggestions are those of that track, requested once per source, when the up next tab opens or when the last track of the queue starts, whichever comes first, and kept while the source plays. A tap on a related song plays that list from it, under the source "Songs like <the current title>". The player has a heart beside the title (shown only for a track that can be liked) and a more button in the header that opens the track menu (`track-menu.md`); every up next and related song row has the same button. A title that does not fit scrolls (a marquee). Playback continues in the background and with the phone locked, and the lock screen (iOS Control Center, Android media notification) shows the track's title, artists, album, large artwork and effective duration, updated on each track. Play and pause from it are native; next and previous run the same `next()` and `previous()` as the player's buttons (autoplay, repeat one and the 3-second restart rule included). The controls are cleared when nothing is loaded or the list is over (stop, sign-out, a failed start, a player error, the end of the list, a start cancelled while resolving); a track the user paused keeps them.

## Platform differences

- iOS 26+: the mini player is `NativeTabs.BottomAccessory`, the system sizes the frame, draws the glass capsule and sets the gap to the tab bar; the mini player draws bare inside it (no own surface, no tint), clipped to the frame.
- Older iOS: the `accessory` of `FloatingTabBar`, at the pill's width (the window width minus `layout.gutter` on each side), `spacing.xs` above the pill.
- Android: the `accessory` of `FloatingTabBar` inside the docked bar (ADR 035), the window width minus `spacing.sm` on each side, `spacing.xs` above the tabs.
- iOS: no close button; the player closes by dragging down, or with the VoiceOver escape gesture.
- The sheet handle: 48 x 5 in white at 45% on Android (`layout.handleWidthAndroid`, `layout.handleHeightAndroid`, `color.overlay.handleAndroid`); iOS keeps `layout.handleWidth` x `layout.handleHeight` in `color.overlay.muted`. Its position is the same row on both.
- Android: the chevron-down close button stays, next to the drag; the system back closes too. The header's more button draws three vertical dots (ADR 027).
- Android, where the column scrolls: a drag that starts inside the column may be taken by the scroll; the header always closes.
- The sheet's selector: on Android, Material 3 primary tabs across the full width (`layout.controlHeight` tabs, no divider, and the press is the fade to `motion.pressOpacity`, the selected label in `typography.subtitle`, a `layout.tabIndicatorHeightAndroid` indicator in the cover's `image.color` mixed with white at `color.mix.tabIndicator` (`color.accent.primary` without a color) with `radius.full` top corners, as wide as the selected label, sliding over `motion.duration.base`, without sliding under reduce motion); iOS keeps the pill `SegmentedControl` with `layout.gutter` at the sides.
- Background playback (`shouldPlayInBackground`), `playsInSilentMode` and `doNotMix` are on, with the `expo-audio` config plugin (background playback on, microphone off). iOS shows the lock screen and Control Center; Android shows the media notification with one previous and one next button. The engine is created once and kept across tracks, paused between them, and released on stop; its audio session stays active while paused.

Autoplay: the switch is a `SwitchRow` at the top of up next (on Android the Material 3 switch, see DESIGN.md, Switch; iOS keeps the native `Switch`), `layout.gutter` sides, `spacing.sm` above and below. A failed read of the setting draws `FailureState` (the server cause, `common:error.server.*`) with `common:retry` in its place and leaves autoplay off; a failed write draws a `common:error.generic` line under the row (`ErrorText`: announced by VoiceOver when it appears and again on each new attempt, a polite live region on Android). It is on by default on a fresh install and stored on the device under `beatly-autoplay` (`on` or `off`; anything else reads as on and is logged). With autoplay on, the end of the last track of the play order starts the suggestions as a new source, like a tap on the first one, under the track source of the track the previous source started on. Each new source brings its own suggestions, so autoplay goes on while there are any. With autoplay off, with no suggestions (`data.tracks: []` or only the requested track), while they are still loading (they are requested when the last track starts, so a last track that ends before they arrive pauses) or after they failed, the end pauses at 0 as below. Repeat one replays and never autoplays. With shuffle on, autoplay starts once every track of the shuffled order has played; the first suggestion plays first and the rest follow shuffled. Next at the last track does what the end of the last track does: with autoplay on and the suggestions loaded it starts the first one under the same track source; with autoplay off or no suggestions loaded it does nothing. Next ignores repeat one, as on any other track. With autoplay off the tab draws only the rest of the queue: no divider, no suggestions, nor their loading or error; the request is unchanged, and nothing left in the queue draws the expected empty state. The divider is a `border.hairline` line in `color.surface.border`, `layout.gutter` from the sides, with no vertical margin, drawn only when both the rest and the suggestions have rows. Previous at the first suggestion restarts it, because the finished source is no longer the queue.

The player cover draws the track's `image` at `large`; the up next and related rows draw `small`, the related carousels `medium`. The wash is the track's `image.color`, as the source response carried it when the queue was built (ADR 030); a track change from no color to a color fades in over `motion.duration.base`. `PlayableTrack.coverUrl` is only the legacy URL the write routes take (likes, plays, playlist adds); it is never drawn. On every track change, with the player open or not, the next track of the queue has its cover prefetched at the player cover's tier into the memory and disk cache (no API route); a failure is logged and drawn nowhere. The first autoplay suggestion is not in the queue and is not prefetched.

Play after the list ended: when the last track ends the controller pauses on it at position 0; `toggle` (the detail play button, the player sheet and the mini player alike) then restarts the list from position 0 of the current play order (list order with shuffle off, the shuffled order with shuffle on) instead of replaying only the last track. A pause while the list is still running resumes in place.

The up next and related song rows mark the current track by id (the now playing bars over the cover, no row background, frozen while paused, static under reduce motion); in up next, whose rest is the queue after
the current track, only a later repeat of the current track can match.

The sheet's own keys are `player:sheet.open`, `player:sheet.close`, `player:sheet.tabs.upNext`, `player:sheet.tabs.lyrics` and
`player:sheet.tabs.related`; loading uses `common:loading`.

### Playback status strip

The mini player keeps its look on a failure: the subtitle is always the artists. A failed track, or no connection, draws one `StatusStrip` (see `tabs.md`) above the mini player: `player:strip.offline` (no connection, or a `network` failure), `player:strip.slow` (a `timeout`), `player:strip.failed` (any other). Tapping it asks the controller to retry. Nothing retries a failed track by itself when the connection returns, and the strip stays until it is tapped or another track starts. Play all of a paged list (own and liked playlists) started offline with pages left records a play-start failure (`connection/playStart.ts`) and draws the same offline strip without waiting on a paused page; tapping it runs the play all again (see `playlist.md`).

## Data

The player registers plays: after 30 seconds of real listening of one listen (position steps while playing; a step above 2 s or backwards is a seek and does not count; a pause stops the count; a listen starts on every track start, every repeat-one replay and after the list ends) `core`'s listening counter sends `POST /plays` once (`private, no-cache`; reasons `invalid_request`, `unauthorized`, `upstream_error`, `upstream_timeout`; branches on none). `album` and `album_id` come from the track, or from the album being played; a track without album, without cover or without a first artist id is not registered; every artist is sent, a later one with `id: null` included. A failure is logged and never touches playback. Every failure the controller enters (the transition into failed) is reported once by `core`'s playback error reporter with `POST /errors/playback` (`no-store`; reasons `invalid_request`, `unauthorized`, `rate_limited`, `upstream_error`, `upstream_timeout`; branches on none). A resolution failure is sent as `stage` `resolve` with its cause as `error_code`; a player failure as `stage` `playback`, `error_code` `playback`. `error_message` is a fixed English text per code. `http_status` is sent only for an `unplayable` non-2xx stream answer, and only the number. `platform` and `os_version` come from the OS (the Android release, not the API level); `app_version` is the app's config version (`unknown` if missing). Text is trimmed and cut to the backend's limits. Nothing is sent signed out. A failed report, 429 included, is dropped with a debug log: no retry, nothing drawn, playback untouched. Besides `POST /plays`, this report and the up next read when the last track of the queue starts, the player calls no other Beatly API route. The sheet reads three, each only when its tab is opened, not paginated, cached
per track (up next: per track and source pair) for the `Cache-Control: max-age` they send (at most 6 h, 24 h and 12 h): `GET /tracks/{id}/upnext`,
`GET /tracks/{id}/lyrics` and `GET /tracks/{id}/related`. The reasons listed are `track_not_found`, `invalid_request` (422, up next only), `unauthorized`,
`upstream_error` and `upstream_timeout`; the sheet branches on none: every failure is `common:error.generic` with retry. `GET /tracks/{id}/upnext` carries `source_kind` and `source_id` when the source is an album (`album`, the album id), one of the caller's own playlists (`playlist`, its id), a genre playlist (`genre_playlist`, its id) or the liked songs (`liked`, `liked`). Under an artist, a search, a tap on a suggestion or a related song, or autoplay's suggestions (a track source), it carries neither. The backend leaves the source's tracks and the ones played in the last 24 h out of the suggestions. The entry is cached per track and source pair, so the same track under two sources is two entries, and renaming the playing playlist keeps it. Up next, for the track the source started on, is also read when the last track of the queue starts, sheet open or not, so autoplay has the suggestions; either way it is read once per source, and the tab never refetches it. The tab reads it with `refetchOnMount: false`, the first hook in the repo to override the mount refetch: the list must stay fixed through a source, so a stale entry is drawn as is and only the root observer revalidates it, at the first trigger; the cache time still comes from `Cache-Control`. That root observer stays mounted until the next source, so the entry is not garbage-collected (5 minutes by default) and fetched a second time, possibly with a different list, at the last-track trigger. The first
up next track is the requested one and is not drawn. The nudged tracks are kept in memory by `core`'s sheet nudge service, by track id, for the app's session; nothing is stored, so after a restart every track nudges again. The decision is taken once per opening of the player, for the track current when it opens: a track change while the player is open does not nudge. An opening under reduce motion marks its track too (only the animation is skipped). The service is created once by `createCore()`, so the set lasts the app session and resets on restart; `useSheetNudge` lives in `src/screens/player/` because it is a synchronous read of that service, not a query hook. Accepted gap: `useReduceMotion` starts `false`, so under system reduce motion a first opening on a track can start the bounce and stop it a few ms later, with the handle parked at rest; the track still counts as nudged.

The stream URL is resolved on the device by `core`'s stream resolver (ADR 021): a POST to the
endpoint in `EXPO_PUBLIC_STREAM_ENDPOINT` with a 15-second timeout, carrying fixed locale fields and the identifier the endpoint last issued (kept in storage), taking the highest-bitrate `audio/mp4` on iOS and
`audio/mp4`, then `audio/webm`, on Android. A login-required refusal is retried once with a fresh identifier, or none; a second refusal is the error state. Nothing caches the URL; each start resolves again. The typed failure
(`unplayable`, `timeout`, `network`, `invalid_response`, or `playback` from the engine) maps to a cause: `network` to offline, `timeout` to slow, the rest to server (`playbackFailureCause`). The player screen draws that cause with `FailureState`; for offline its body is `player:error.offline`, which asks for a tap on Retry, because a playback failure never retries by itself. Offline, any start fails at once with `network` (`resolveOnlyOnline` wraps the resolver in `createCore`), instead of waiting for the resolver's timeout. The queue is the tracks loaded when the row is tapped: a paginated playlist stops at its
loaded pages.

`PlayableTrack.artists` stays a plain list of artist refs, not a non-empty tuple, while the API schemas enforce the track artists rule at the edge (first artist with an id). A tuple would break about ten typed fixtures for no runtime gain, and legacy rows of the likes mirror may hold an empty list or a null first id. So the first-artist guard is repeated inline, with no shared helper, in four places: `likeInputOf` (`packages/core/src/services/likes.ts`), `addTrackInputOf` (`packages/core/src/services/playlists.ts`), the listening counter (`packages/core/src/services/listening.ts`) and `trackMenuItems` (`apps/mobile/src/screens/trackMenu/trackMenuItems.ts`).

Duration: the resolver returns the chosen format's duration (`approxDurationMs`). The controller's duration is the stream's, else the track's `duration_seconds` from the backend, else the engine's; an engine progress never replaces a known one. The track ends when its position is within `END_TOLERANCE_SECONDS` (one 100 ms tick) of that duration, the same way as the engine's `ended`, whichever comes first, and only once. The seek and the seek bar are bounded by it, and `POST /plays` sends it rounded to whole seconds. The order applies to both platforms, as the issue chose: without the stream's duration, Android too ends at the backend's whole-second `duration_seconds`, up to about a second early or late, and that is accepted. Like and playlist-add keep sending the catalog's `duration_seconds`, not the effective one, because the catalog metadata of a row must not depend on whether that track is playing. Why: measured on a phone (Expo Go, SDK 58, expo-audio 58.0.10) with fragmented MP4 AAC streams, iOS's engine reports exactly twice the real duration from the first status and never corrects it, while position runs at 1x and seeks land exactly, and its end event arrives only at the doubled duration, leaving that much silence; Android reports the real one. The lock screen shows the effective duration through the expo-audio patch (ADR 037); without a known duration it falls back to the engine's (doubled on iOS), and Expo Go, which runs the unpatched module, shows the engine's.

## Navigation

`/player`, a see-through modal (`transparentModal`) above the tabs that slides up, opened from the mini player. A downward drag from the header, or from the column at its top, closes it; a short one springs back. Close pops, or replaces with `/`. Under reduce motion the route fades in and out and the drag does not move the player. With the sheet open, Android back and the VoiceOver escape close the sheet first, with its reverse motion, and the player stays; a second back or escape closes the player.

An artist or an album of Related, or Go to artist and Go to album of the track menu, closes the player and pushes `/artist/[id]` or `/album/[id]` in the current tab.

## i18n namespace

`player`, `trackMenu` (the heart's labels and the menu) and `common`.
